ATLANTA Investment Banker's Shiba Inu Swallows Hardware Wallet Holding $13 Million

ATLANTA, GEORGIA — For 36-year-old investment banker Kyle Shickner, Tuesday night began like any other: checking his Ledger Nano X hardware wallet at his Alpharetta apartment while his three-year-old Shiba Inu, Tyler, dozed nearby on the couch. By midnight, Shickner says, the device — which secured roughly $13 million in cryptocurrency, the bulk of it a long-held Binance Coin position he says he never intended to trade — was gone, and Tyler was gagging on the living room floor.

"I stepped away to grab a coffee, maybe a minute, tops," Shickner said in a phone interview. "When I came back, the Ledger was gone and Tyler had this look on his face. I've had Tyler three years. I know that look."

Shickner says he began accumulating Binance Coin in 2019, treating it as a long-term holding rather than a trading position, and kept it on hardware storage rather than an exchange for security reasons — the position that ultimately grew into the $13 million balance stored on the device.

Foreign body ingestion is among the most common reasons dogs are brought into emergency veterinary care, and small electronics — remote controls, phone chargers, hearing aid cases — are frequent culprits because of their size and texture. The Ledger Nano X is roughly the size and shape of a car key fob, small enough that veterinary staff say it's plausible Tyler mistook it for a chew toy in an unsupervised moment, a pattern behaviorists sometimes describe as opportunistic ingestion in food-motivated breeds.

Shickner brought Tyler to Alpharetta Animal Hospital just before midnight, where an X-ray confirmed a rectangular object lodged in his stomach. Dr. Alex Davenport, the emergency veterinarian who examined him, said the call on how to proceed did not come easily.

"Normally with a foreign body like this, survival surgery is the standard recommendation, and Tyler probably would have tolerated it fine," Dr. Davenport said. "But Mr. Shickner was very direct that every hour the device sat in gastric acid was an hour closer to whatever was on that chip being unreadable. Survival surgery means anesthesia, prep, recovery, monitoring — hours we didn't have if the goal was getting that chip out before it failed. Humane euthanasia followed immediately by necropsy was, bluntly, the fastest way to retrieve it."

Shickner says he made the decision on the spot, in the exam room. "That was the worst ten minutes of my life, honestly," he said. "Tyler wasn't just a pet to me. But she walked me through it — every hour mattered for the chip — and I had to make a call. It was quick, and she told me it was painless. I keep telling myself that."

The device was recovered within the hour — a small rectangle of brushed metal that Shickner says he held in his palm for almost a minute, braced for good news. A hardware-forensics technician Shickner consulted for a second opinion said the timeline worked against him. "Twelve hours or so is right around the edge of the safe window for these enclosures against gastric exposure," said the technician, who asked not to be named because he was not authorized to speak on behalf of his employer. "He landed on the wrong side of it — the seal didn't hold." When Shickner connected the wallet to his laptop that night, it failed to power on at all, which the technician said is itself a strong sign the secure element — the chip that stores a wallet's private keys — had been compromised beyond recovery.

Shickner says he kept no backup of the wallet's seed phrase, so the $13 million cannot be recovered by any other means. Ledger has not issued a public statement on the incident; Shickner says he does not plan to send the unit in for further testing, calling the damage "final."

"I keep going back to it," Shickner said. "I thought I was going to save thirteen million dollars. Instead I lost Tyler. And I lost the thirteen million anyway."

Shickner said he's tried to keep the loss in perspective in the days since. He still has his job and his family, he said, and he believes that's ultimately what will get him through it. "I know how lucky I am in a lot of ways," he said. "That doesn't make the specific stuff easier. But I'll come back from this. I have to."

Security specialists say the case underscores a basic precaution many holders skip: anyone storing significant cryptocurrency on a single hardware wallet should keep a written, offline backup of their seed phrase in a separate secure location — the only way to recover funds if the device itself is ever lost, damaged, or destroyed.
